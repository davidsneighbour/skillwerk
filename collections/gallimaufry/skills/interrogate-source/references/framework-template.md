# Framework synthesis template

Use this template as an analytical aid, not as a mandatory output shape.

## Name

Use a descriptive functional name.

Avoid retaining branded terminology unless the terminology itself is important.

## Purpose

What problem does the framework solve?

What decision, transformation, or outcome is it intended to support?

## Inputs

What information, resources, state, evidence, or conditions are required?

Distinguish required inputs from optional context.

## Preconditions and assumptions

What must already be true for the framework to work?

Which assumptions are supported by evidence, and which remain hypotheses?

## Process

Describe the actual mechanism.

Prefer:

* stages;
* flows;
* gates;
* loops;
* decision rules.

Avoid converting every observation into a procedural step.

## Decision logic

Where does the process branch?

What conditions determine what happens next?

Permit outcomes such as:

* stop;
* insufficient evidence;
* no action required;
* escalate;
* reject the premise;
* return for more information.

## Outputs

What concrete result should the framework produce?

An output should be observable or usable.

## Evidence and validation

What evidence supports the conclusions?

How can the output be tested?

What would falsify the framework's conclusion?

## Constraints

What limits the framework?

Examples:

* incomplete information;
* time;
* cost;
* technical constraints;
* legal constraints;
* organisational authority;
* user behaviour;
* market conditions.

## Trade-offs

What improves when this framework is followed?

What becomes slower, more expensive, less flexible, or otherwise worse?

Do not present optimisation as free.

## Success conditions

How do we know the framework worked?

Prefer behavioural or measurable outcomes over subjective impressions.

## When it works

Describe contexts where its assumptions and mechanism are appropriate.

## When it breaks

Describe:

* invalid assumptions;
* failure modes;
* edge cases;
* adversarial conditions;
* contexts where another framework is more appropriate.

## Optional operational dimensions

Add only where relevant.

### Confidence

How certain are the conclusions and why?

### Reversibility

Can decisions be undone cheaply?

### Dependencies

What must happen elsewhere?

### Controls

What prevents incorrect or unsafe execution?

### Exception handling

What happens outside the normal path?

### Governance

Who can decide, approve, override, or stop the process?

### Monitoring

What indicates that assumptions have changed?

### Recovery

How does the system return to a safe or useful state after failure?

### Cost of delay

What happens if no action is taken yet?
