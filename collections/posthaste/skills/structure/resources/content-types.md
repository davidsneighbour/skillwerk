# Content types

Choose by the reader's task and the material actually available. Each sequence is a reusable layout: replace the role names with natural prose or descriptive headings. Evidence may be an attributed source, supplied observation, worked example, or documented result, as appropriate. Optional parts are marked explicitly; other roles are needed for a complete version of that type.

| Type | Choose when | Ordered layout | Facts to request if absent |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| TIL / discovery | Share one useful thing just learned | Optional context; discovery; example where needed to understand or reuse it; optional takeaway | What was learned, and what demonstrates it? Do not claim the author just learned it without support. |
| Problem-led TIL | Explain a small obstacle and its fix | Context; problem; reason or discovery; solution or example; reusable takeaway | Actual obstacle, cause if claimed, and working fix. Omit an unknown cause rather than inventing it. |
| Fact-drop | Share one specific factual discovery | Self-contained fact; essential qualification; source | Reliable support for the fact. A surprising claim alone is insufficient. |
| How-to | Help a competent reader complete a task | Goal; prerequisites; numbered actions; observable verification; optional troubleshooting | Environment, prerequisites, actions, and success condition. Do not invent executable commands or untested outcomes. |
| Tutorial | Teach through a guided experience | Learning outcome; prerequisites; setup; guided actions with explanation; verification; learning recap | Learner level, reproducible setup, exercise, and expected results. |
| Explainer | Help the reader understand why or how | Question; direct answer; necessary definitions; mechanism; evidence or example; limitations; implication | Support for the mechanism and key factual claims. Do not fill missing causation with analogy. |
| Problem-solution | Diagnose a problem and evaluate a remedy | Situation; problem; cause if known; solution; evaluation; optional next step | Problem evidence, remedy, and evaluation basis. Label an untested remedy as proposed. |
| Listicle | Present several distinct useful items | Promise and scope; ordering rule; repeated item plus explanation or evidence; optional synthesis | Supported items and any ranking criteria. Derive the count from the material; never pad. |
| Comparison | Help choose between alternatives | Decision context; shared criteria; each option against the same criteria; trade-offs; conditional recommendation | Alternatives, priorities, and comparable evidence. Mark unavailable comparisons explicitly. |
| Product review | Judge suitability from evidence | Verdict and audience fit; evaluated version and conditions; criteria; strengths and weaknesses with evidence; alternatives where supported; recommendation | Testing or evaluation basis, version, and relevant constraints. Distinguish hands-on experience from desk research. |
| Op-ed / argument | Defend an explicit position | Occasion if relevant; thesis; supporting reasons and evidence; strongest relevant objection; concession or rebuttal; implication | Author's position, factual support, and grounds for responding to objections. |
| Longform essay | Explore an idea through sustained thought | Opening tension or scene; inquiry or developing thesis; connected movements with evidence; complication; earned ending | Material that sustains the inquiry. A delayed thesis is allowed; do not inflate a short point into an essay. |
| Case study | Report a concrete intervention and outcome | Result preview when known; case context; challenge; intervention; implementation where needed; results with evidence and limits; lessons | Actual intervention, outcome, measurement basis, and period. A plan without results is not a success story. |
| Personal narrative / lesson | Make experience meaningful to others | Hook or abstract; people/time/place; complicating event; why it mattered; resolution; present-day lesson | Actual events, author involvement, and outcome. Evaluation may appear throughout. |
| News / inverted pyramid | Report a new development | Headline; lead with key event and known who/what/when/where; significance; attributed detail; background; optional next development | Event, date, actors, and attribution. Unknown why/how stays unknown. |
| Announcement / press release | Communicate an organisation's news | Headline; key announcement and availability; supporting details; optional approved quote; practical next step; boilerplate/contact only for a formal release | What changes, for whom, when, and where to act. Quotes require supplied or approved wording. |
| Feature / nut graf | Develop reported material through a scene | Documented scene or anecdote; early paragraph explaining the story's point; reporting and context; wider meaning; resonant close | Sourced scene, reporting, and attribution. Never invent dialogue or a composite person. |
| Q&A / FAQ | Answer real reader questions or present an interview | Scope; repeated genuine question, direct answer, supporting detail, and relevant exception | Actual questions and supported answers. Do not fabricate interview quotations or turn unrelated information into artificial questions. |
| Research summary / academic summary | Translate an existing study for readers | Question and significance; study/source identification; method; findings; strength and limits; proportionate implications; source links | Study text, design, findings, and limitations. Do not rely on a headline or imply causality beyond the design. |
| IMRaD | Report original research | Introduction; methods; results; discussion, including limitations and interpretation; references | Actual study methods and results. A summary of someone else's research normally needs the research-summary layout instead. |

Blog post, social post, thread, and newsletter are delivery formats, not competing content types. For example, choose `case study + before-after-bridge + social post` rather than inventing a separate type for every combination.
