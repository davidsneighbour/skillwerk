# Reasoning patterns

Use a pattern only where it helps organise the supplied material. It can shape an opening, a section, or the whole draft. Roles may be implicit in prose. Persuasion does not justify stronger claims than the evidence supports.

| Pattern | Suitable use | Ordered roles | Guard against |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| AIDA | A supported offer or request for action | Attention; interest; desire grounded in actual benefit; action | Invented benefits, artificial scarcity, or adding a sales objective to an informational draft. |
| PAS | A recognised problem with demonstrated consequences | Problem; concrete consequences; solution | Exaggerated pain or urgency. Agitation means explaining consequences, not inventing fear. |
| BAB / before-after-bridge | A transformation or proposed route | Before; after; bridge connecting the states | Presenting an imagined future as an achieved result. |
| SCQA | A question arising from changed circumstances | Situation; complication; explicit or implied question; answer | Long background before the point, or a question the draft does not answer. |
| Minto Pyramid | A recommendation supported by grouped reasons | Answer; distinct reason groups; evidence for each; implications and risks | Arbitrary groups or reasons that do not support the answer. SCQA can introduce the question. |
| Monroe's motivated sequence | Evidence-based advocacy | Attention; need; satisfaction/remedy; realistic future consequences; action | Speculative outcomes stated as certain, or unsupported emotional pressure. |
| Toulmin | An argument that needs its reasoning made explicit | Claim; grounds; warrant explaining the connection; backing where needed; qualifier; rebuttal | Treating evidence as self-explanatory or deleting qualifications. |
| Classical argument | A position requiring context and objection handling | Introduction; background; proposition; proof; refutation or concession; conclusion | A straw-man counterargument. |
| Rogerian argument | Disagreement where common ground is useful | Fair account of competing view; shared ground; circumstances where each view applies; author's position; workable accommodation | Inventing agreement or weakening a material disagreement. |
| Claim-evidence-reasoning | A compact analytical section | Claim; supporting evidence; explanation of why it supports the claim | Evidence that is adjacent to the claim but does not establish it. |
| Problem-solution / SPSE | Diagnosis and remedy | Situation; problem; solution; evaluation | Calling an untested remedy effective. Cause can be added only when supported. |
| Labov narrative | Personal experience with a transferable lesson | Abstract; orientation; complicating action; evaluation; resolution; coda returning to the present | Invented experience. Evaluation can run through the story. |
| Chronology | Order or dependency is the point | Starting state; ordered events or actions; outcome; significance | Inferring causation simply from sequence. |
| Inverted pyramid | Readers need the news or answer immediately | Most important finding; supporting details; background in decreasing importance | Burying essential qualifications where cutting the ending would remove them. |

Use the content-type layout for required evidence. A pattern changes the arrangement; it does not supply missing information. For instance, BAB can organise a case study, but it cannot replace actual results. A narrative arc or delayed essay thesis is a valid alternative to front-loading when discovery is the reader's purpose.
