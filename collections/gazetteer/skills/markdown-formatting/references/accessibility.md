# Markdown accessibility review

Review the document in its publishing context. Inspect lint coverage first, then assess meaning and structure; do not assume a particular configuration, rule set, or commit hook is installed. Passing mechanical checks does not establish that the content is useful to its readers.

## Images and alternatives

* Inspect the image and surrounding text before judging or rewriting its alternative. Describe its purpose and relevant information, not its filename or a placeholder such as "image". Avoid redundant "image of" wording; name the medium when that matters.
* For a linked image, describe its destination or action when that is its purpose. For a purely decorative image, an empty alternative can be correct. Do not invent descriptive text solely to satisfy a non-empty-alt lint rule; report a conflict with project lint policy and agree an appropriate treatment.
* Give complex charts and diagrams a short identifying alternative plus an accessible longer explanation or data table in the document or linked content. Use a disclosure only when the renderer supports it and its content remains accessible.
* If the image or essential context is unavailable, mark the proposed alternative as uncertain and ask for the missing information. Do not infer image contents from the filename.

See the W3C WAI guidance on [decorative images](https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/images/decorative/), [functional images](https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/images/functional/), and [complex images](https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/images/complex/).

## Structure and language

* Check heading levels against the final page hierarchy, including titles generated by the template. Consecutive levels can still represent the wrong nesting. Do not add another first-level heading without checking the containing page.
* Use real list syntax for related items and steps, rather than paragraphs made to look like lists with symbols. Use ordered lists when sequence matters.
* Check that link text explains the destination or purpose in context, rather than merely passing a banned-word rule.
* Check jargon, dense paragraphs, and unnecessarily complex wording against the intended audience. Recommend simpler language where it improves understanding while preserving technical meaning and the author's tone.
* Keep meaningful information in words as well as emoji or symbols. Flag repeated decorative emoji that can make spoken output noisy; pronunciation and verbosity vary with assistive technology. Do not replace meaningful text with an icon alone.

## Report and edit

For each finding, give the file and location, the evidence, the effect on readers, and a specific proposed correction. Distinguish confirmed defects from uncertain alternatives and editorial recommendations. Make edits within the user's authorised scope; a review alone does not authorise rewriting. Approval to remediate does not require another approval for each wording change unless essential meaning remains uncertain.

Run the configured Markdown checks for edited files. Verify the rendered structure when practical, and state any gaps. Do not claim screen-reader verification from source inspection or lint results.
